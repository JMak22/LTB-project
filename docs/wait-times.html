Average Days to First Hearing

This dashboard presents provincial averages, published by the LTB, for single application types.

For detailed longitudinal information, I wish you luck with the combined CSV of all quarters, available in my repo. I haven't been able to find a way to tease out anything simple or clear about wait times.

Reconciling LTB's published data, changing performance targets and the variety of wait times for single applications vs application combinations makes it complicated to present a simple picture of how long will it take until the first hearing for a specific application. That will depend on the individual situation itself.

Furthermore, LTB's own performance targets keep changing and they're not the same across application classes. For example, the performance target for L1 or L9 applications was 50 days up until recently (Nov 2026), but has since changed to 35 days. All other application types had a target of 55 days, which was changed to 40 days.

Note that, even though Q1 2026-27 data was published, there's a quality issue with it that prevents us from sharing it as we don't know the actual dates that the data refers to. Specifically, L1&L1 application averages in the spreadsheet are

How to read this

Each bar is the average number of days until a first hearing, across Ontario, as published by Tribunals Ontario. An average is not a promise. Scheduling varies, so check the Tribunals Ontario portal for your own file.

How each year is worked out

Tribunals Ontario publishes these averages one quarter at a time and does not publish how many applications are behind them. For each year, this page takes the simple average of the quarterly averages that were published. It is not weighted by the number of applications, so it can differ from the true average for the year. Some quarters are missing for some application types, and some quarterly values look very low or very high, so one quarter can move a year a lot. The Inspect the data table shows the quarterly averages behind every number. For 2015 to 16, only a full year figure was published.

What is not shown

Many people file more than one type of application at the same time. Tribunals Ontario reports those combinations on their own, so the bars show only single type L1, L9, T1 and T6 applications. Other types and combinations are in the Inspect the data table. Regional averages are not shown. The share of hearings held within a standard is not shown, because the published figures for it are not consistent.

A note on the data

The Tribunals Ontario files do not say whether the days are calendar days or business days. The Excel files state a standard of 25 business days for L1 and L9 and 30 business days for other applications. The Tribunals Ontario Key Performance Indicators page lists 50 calendar days for L1 and L9 and 55 calendar days for other applications. These do not match, so this page does not compare the averages with any standard. The latest period on this page is Q4 2025 to 26 (January 1 to March 31, 2026), the most recent data we could confirm.
